## The shape of it

Stephen walks along the strand. He thinks. He sits on a rock and thinks some
more. He watches a dog, and some cockle-pickers, and the tide. He writes
something down on a scrap of paper. He walks off.

That is all the plot there is, and this is where a great many first readings
end.

## The trick

The trick is **removal of the frame**. In the first two episodes, Stephen's
interior voice was suspended inside a narration that told you where he was and
who was speaking. Here the frame is gone. The episode is almost entirely inside
his head, and his head is erudite, allusive, and moving fast between languages
and registers. Nothing pauses to orient you.

The result is that you cannot read this episode the way you read the first two,
and the book has arranged for you to discover that.

Two things helped me:

**Read it aloud, or subvocalise.** A surprising amount of what scans as
impenetrable on the page resolves as rhythm — it is a man muttering to himself
on a beach, and the muttering has a beat.

**Read for the physical scene.** Underneath the associations, a completely
concrete sequence is occurring: he walks, he sits, he notices a dog, the tide
comes in, he leaves. Track that, and let the philosophy wash. On a second pass
the philosophy attaches itself to the walking.

The title's shape-shifter is the organising joke: everything in the episode
changes form under attention. Sand was rock; the dog becomes several other
animals in Stephen's description of it; a drowned man will surface later.
Nothing holds its shape, including the prose.

## Threads

**Seeing, and its limits.** The episode opens on a small experiment in
perception — eyes shut, walking anyway — and the question of what the world is
doing when you are not looking runs through everything after.

**The drowned man.** Mentioned here, mentioned again several times across the
day. Small, easy to miss, and it accumulates weight.

**Stephen's unwritten work.** He writes a fragment on a scrap of paper. The
book is quietly interested in the gap between a person who intends to be an
artist and one who is producing anything.

**Mother, again.** Second episode running. The grief is not moving.

## Loose ends

I do not think I have read this episode properly yet, and I have read it four
times. Each pass surfaces a different organising idea and I no longer trust any
of them individually.

What I am fairly confident about is placement. Putting the hardest early
episode third, immediately before the book switches to a much warmer and more
legible character, is a deliberate piece of engineering. Get through this and
the fourth episode arrives like a window opening. If you are stuck here, that
is the argument for pushing on: relief is one episode away, and it is
substantial.

**Next:** episode four. A different man, a different house, breakfast — and the
book becomes, briefly, very easy to love.
