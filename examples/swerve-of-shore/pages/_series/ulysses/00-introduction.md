Nearly everyone who gives up on this book gives up in one of three places: page
forty, page two hundred, or somewhere in the long theatrical stretch in the
middle. That is oddly encouraging. It means the difficulty is structural rather
than personal — the book has a small number of specific walls, and you can see
each of them coming.

## The plan

**One episode at a time, in order, no skipping.** Eighteen episodes, wildly
uneven in length. Treat each as a unit and stop at the end of it, even if you
have momentum. The episodes are written in different modes, and running two
together makes the seams harder to feel.

**Read it through once before reading about it.** A first pass at reading speed
— confused, partial, missing most of the jokes — is worth more than a slow pass
propped up by a companion volume. You cannot notice a pattern that a footnote
noticed for you.

**Then read about it, then read it again.** The second pass is where the book
opens. This is the part people skip, and it is the whole method.

**Write something down each time.** Not analysis. A paragraph on what happened,
a note on what confused you. The point is to have a record of your own
confusion, because the confusion is data — it is usually the book doing
something deliberate.

## What the three walls actually are

The first wall is tonal. The opening moves fast between registers, and it does
not stop to tell you who anyone is or why the room is tense. You are meant to
be slightly at sea. Keep going; the ground firms up considerably by the fourth
episode.

The second wall is the interior voice. Around the middle of the book the prose
starts spending long stretches inside a character's head with no signposting,
so the sentences stop behaving like narration. The trick is to stop trying to
parse every clause and start reading for the drift of attention — what the mind
snags on, and how it moves off again.

The third wall is the one that gets talked about most, and it is genuinely
hard: a long central episode written as something other than a novel. There is
no way through it but through it, and the second reading is where it pays.

## What I will do here

Work the plan in public. Each episode gets an entry with the same four
headings — the shape of it, the trick, the threads, the loose ends — so the
entries stay comparable and the running motifs are easy to trace across
episodes.

The loose ends are the part I care about most. Guides tend to present a tidy
book. This one is not tidy, and pretending otherwise makes it harder to read,
not easier.
